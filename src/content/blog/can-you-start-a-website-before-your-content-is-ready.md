Yes. You can start planning a small business website before the final wording, logo and photos are ready. Rough notes are enough to decide what the site needs, how its pages fit together and what still needs to be supplied before launch.

Starting early can also reveal missing decisions while there is still time to make them.

## Bring the minimum business details

To begin, write down:

- your business name and what the business does
- the main services or products you want to promote
- the customers and locations you serve
- the action you want a visitor to take
- a phone number or email address for project discussions
- any date or event that affects timing

The visitor's next step matters most. It might be calling, sending an enquiry or using a booking service you already have. That choice shapes the content and the website layout.

Tell the person building the site which details are confirmed and which are still undecided. Estimates, draft prices and unapproved claims should not be presented as final facts.

## Work can begin with rough copy

Bullet points can show what each service covers, who it is for and what customers often ask. They can be organised into a page plan and turned into draft website copy.

You do not need to invent polished slogans before the project starts. Clear facts are more useful. Final wording still needs your review because you know the business, its prices and its promises to customers.

## Photos and branding can follow

Existing photos and a logo help, provided you have permission to use them and the files are suitable for the website. If they are not ready, the project can still begin with a list of the images each page needs.

Agree how missing images will be handled. Options may include supplying photos later, arranging new photography or licensing suitable stock images. Logo design and photography should be included in the agreed scope if they are part of the job.

## Some decisions must be made before launch

Planning and design can move ahead while material is being prepared. Launch still depends on confirmed business details, approved wording, usable images and a working customer contact method.

The domain, email accounts and any booking or enquiry services also need agreed owners and access. Do not send passwords or recovery codes in an initial enquiry. Access can be arranged after responsibilities and scope are confirmed.

## Review the site in one clear round

Ask for a complete draft, then review it as a customer would. Check names, phone numbers, email addresses, service areas, prices, claims and links. Confirm that the main next step is easy to find on a phone as well as a computer.

Collect feedback from everyone who needs to approve the site and return one consolidated list. Mark each item as a correction, a requested change or a new idea. New pages or features may change the cost or launch date, so agree to them before extra work begins.

Once the final facts and content are approved, the site can be tested and prepared for launch.
