A website and business email usually have two kinds of cost. You pay once for the work needed to set them up, then pay recurring charges to keep the services running.

The yearly total depends on the services you choose, the number of email users and what support you need. A useful quote should separate these costs so you can see what you are agreeing to.

## Setup costs happen at the start

Setup work may include planning the site, writing or editing content, design, development, domain and email configuration, testing and a handover. Photography, logo design, online stores, booking systems and other specialist features may cost extra.

Ask what the setup fee covers and how many pages, features and review rounds are included. The quote should also explain how work outside that scope will be approved and charged.

## Allow for recurring services

Common running costs include:

- **Domain renewal.** This keeps your website address registered. Some domains renew every year, while others can use a different term.
- **Website hosting.** This keeps the website available online. The service and billing period depend on how the site is built and managed.
- **Business email.** Email services commonly charge a subscription for each user or mailbox. Adding staff can increase the total.
- **Optional software.** Booking tools, forms, online stores, security services and other features may have their own subscriptions.
- **Maintenance and changes.** Updates, support and new content may be covered by a plan or quoted when needed.

Do not rely on a single first-year total. Ask what renews, when it renews and whether an introductory rate changes later. Current provider charges should appear in the quote or be checked with the provider before you approve the work.

## Keep ownership clear

Your business should know who owns the domain, website and email accounts. Where practical, register business services in the business's name and use an address the business controls for billing and recovery.

Ask who can access each account, who receives renewal notices and what happens if you change suppliers. Keep copies of invoices, account details and recovery information somewhere secure. Never send passwords or recovery codes through a website enquiry form.

## Use this quote checklist

Before accepting a website and email quote, check that it answers these questions:

1. What work does the setup fee include?
2. Which third-party services will I pay for?
3. How often does each service renew, and who sends the bill?
4. Does the email cost change when I add another user?
5. Who owns and controls the domain, website and email accounts?
6. What support is included after launch?
7. How will later changes or work outside the original scope be quoted?
8. What information and material do I need to provide?

A clear answer to each question makes it easier to compare quotes and budget beyond launch.
